  A Variable-Precision Emulation Study of a Small GPT-2}

\begin{abstract}%
  Should low-precision transformer inference use stochastic rounding~(SR) or
  round-to-nearest~(RN)? Holding the numerical format fixed and varying only the
  rounding rule at operation sites, we find that the answer is site-dependent.

  Two partial analyses account for this. A probabilistic forward- error bound for
  linear projections shows that SR's error envelope grows as
  $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{n}\,u)$ in the reduction length $n$, against
  $\mathcal{O}(n\,u)$ for RN, a gap widest in the long multilayer perceptron (MLP)
  down-projection. A second-order decomposition of the expected cross- entropy
  change at the softmax separates signed drift, drift curvature, and a
  Fisher-weighted variance penalty: MLP noise is mostly a uniform logit shift, to
  which the softmax is invariant, whereas head noise is non-uniform.

  To test this,
  we extend PRISM, a vectorized CPU rounding library, with a variable-precision SR
  algorithm proven exact in hardware floating point. On DistilGPT-2 at \(t=6\)
  significand bits, SR in the MLP raises perplexity to $\times1.15$ the
  full-precision reference, against $\times2.21$ for RN. At the language-model
  head the ordering reverses, since only SR incurs the variance penalty. With the
  MLP output at $t=6$, assigning SR to the MLP and RN to the head brings
  perplexity within $\times1.10$ of the reference, $28\%$ below matched-bit RN.
\end{abstract}
